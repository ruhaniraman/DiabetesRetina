# New vessels (neovascularisation): suspicion model

Generated by `validation/nv_eval.py`. No problem-statement dataset has pixel masks for new vessels, so the model is judged against the **proliferative grade** (PDR = new vessels or pre-retinal/vitreous haemorrhage). NV features (`stage2_structure/nv/nvFeatures.m`): fine, densely branching vessels on/near the optic disc and elsewhere, and large haemorrhages. Logistic regression fitted on 747 training photographs (249 PDR); evaluated once on the held-out test photographs.

**Reading this.** The NV features carry real signal for proliferative disease (AUC well above 0.5 on both test sets), and the strongest feature is the one the anatomy predicts: short, tangled vessel segments. But they are weaker than the CNN's own P(Proliferate_DR) everywhere, and averaging the two does not help. The NV suspicion score is therefore **not shown in the app**; it is kept as a validated research component. Detecting new vessels properly needs pixel-level NV annotations (none of the problem-statement datasets has them; FGADR does, on request).

Largest standardised coefficients (positive = more suspicious of PDR): meanSegmentLength -0.97, fineDensityElsewhere -0.61, branchDensity -0.58, fineDensity +0.42, heArea +0.42, vesselDensity +0.34.

**What this is and is not.** It is an image-level suspicion of proliferative disease built from vessel and haemorrhage features, checked against grades. It does not outline new vessels, and a high score is not a finding: new vessels need confirmation by an ophthalmologist.
