# Stage 4: can the heatmaps be trusted?

Generated by `validation/analyze_gradcam.py` from `validation/gradcam_eval.py` (MATLAB). The map is a Grad-CAM of the **referral score** (P(Moderate)+P(Severe)+P(Proliferate_DR)), computed on the same cropped image that is graded, on a 7x7 grid (each cell is 32 px of the 224 px view).

## 0. Why the map was changed

The referral decision uses the summed referral probability, but the earlier heatmap explained the single most likely *class*. Of the 247 of 548 held-out test eyes that the threshold flags for referral, the most likely class was No_DR or Mild for **51 (21%)**: for those eyes the old map explained a grade that did not drive the decision. The map now explains the referral score itself.

## 1. Sanity check: randomise the network's weights

Correlation (inside the retina) between the real map and the map from a network with random weights. A trustworthy explanation should look **different** (correlation near 0). Median of the absolute values, over eyes where both maps are non-empty.

| Randomised part | Pearson (median abs) | Spearman (median abs) | Eyes compared |
|---|---|---|---|
| Classifier layer only (2 seeds) | 0.22 | 0.20 | 146 of 180 |
| Every layer (2 seeds) | 0.15 | 0.13 | 146 of 180 |

## 2. Deletion with a neutral fill

Cells of the 7x7 grid inside the retina are replaced by the retina's mean colour; the table is the fall in the referral score (positive = the score dropped), averaged over eyes the model clearly flags (referral score >= 0.5). A faithful map: hottest > random > coldest.

| Cells replaced | Hottest | Random (average of 20 draws) | Coldest | Hottest minus random (95% interval) | Eyes where hottest > random |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 of about 38 | 0.015 | -0.035 | -0.045 | +0.050 (+0.020 to +0.087) | 69% (52 eyes) |
| 8 of about 38 | 0.049 | -0.062 | -0.060 | +0.111 (+0.069 to +0.159) | 79% (52 eyes) |
| 12 of about 38 | 0.034 | -0.076 | -0.077 | +0.110 (+0.064 to +0.167) | 90% (52 eyes) |

## 3. Localisation on IDRiD photographs with expert lesion masks

81 of 81 photographs have lesion masks (microaneurysms, haemorrhages, exudates). Pixel-level agreement between the map and the masks, inside the retina. AUC 0.5 is chance. 'Chance' columns are what a randomly placed hottest pixel would score (the share of the retina that is lesion, or within 16 px of a lesion).

| Map | Pixel AUC (median over photographs) | Hottest pixel on a lesion (chance) | Hottest pixel within 16 px of a lesion (chance) | Mean heat on lesions / elsewhere |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Referral map (used by the app) | 0.660 | 12% (3.1%) | 84% (69%) | 1.56 |
| Most-likely-class map (the earlier one) | 0.508 | 4% (3.1%) | 74% (69%) | 1.00 |
| Randomised network (every layer), for comparison | 0.494 | 2% (3.1%) | 52% (69%) | 0.95 |

Optic disc: the disc carries 2% of the referral-map heat (median) while covering 2% of the retina (0.6x its share of the area), so the map is not specially drawn to the disc.

## Reading of the results

- **The map depends on what the network learned.** With random weights the map correlates only 0.15 (all layers) to 0.22 (classifier only) with the real one, so it is not just an edge or brightness map of the image.
- **The hottest cells matter more than other cells, but the effect is modest.** Replacing 12 hottest cells lowers the referral score +0.110 more than 12 random cells (95% interval +0.064 to +0.167; hottest more important in 90% of eyes). In absolute terms removing the hottest cells lowers the score by only 0.034 on average: the evidence for referral is spread over the retina, so a flagged eye stays flagged. (The fill is itself unnatural: replacing random cells RAISES the score, so only the comparison between rows is meaningful.)
- **It is hotter on lesions, weakly.** Pixel AUC 0.66 on lesion masks (0.5 is chance); the hottest pixel is on a lesion in 12% of photographs against 3.1% by chance. It is far from a lesion detector.
- **The earlier map was not usable for localisation:** AUC 0.51 (chance), hottest pixel on a lesion in 4% of photographs. Explaining the referral score fixed that.

## What this does and does not show

- The maps are coarse (a 7x7 grid). They show regions, not lesions.
- Passing these tests would show the map reflects what drives the network's score. It would not show the network reasons like a clinician.
- Localisation uses IDRiD photographs only (the only lesion masks available); deletion and randomisation use APTOS test photographs.
